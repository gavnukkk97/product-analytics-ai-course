### 2. Prioritize with Purpose: Balancing User Value and Business Goals

As product managers, we’re often tasked with balancing user needs with the business’s strategic goals. Not every feature can or should be implemented immediately, and that’s where prioritization frameworks come in.

I use the RICE method—which considers Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort—as a way to prioritize features that will bring the most value to both users and the business. For example, a feature that could have a substantial impact on a large number of users, with a high degree of confidence and manageable development effort, would rank high. But prioritization also involves flexibility. Especially if development resources are limited, I focus on releasing the highest-impact features first, while keeping an eye on lean planning to ensure we stay agile and adaptable.

Balancing user needs with business goals means ensuring that your roadmap reflects high-value work that aligns with both user satisfaction and business growth. By using structured frameworks like RICE, you can bring clarity to prioritization, making each release impactful and aligned with broader objectives.

Key Takeaway: Use prioritization frameworks like RICE to ensure your roadmap delivers high-value features that align with both user satisfaction and business growth.

### 3. Adapt and Improve: Harnessing Feedback for Continuous Growth

User feedback is the cornerstone of product improvement. A product manager’s job doesn’t end after a feature launch; it’s just the beginning. To stay in touch with users’ evolving needs, I gather feedback from multiple channels. These include App Store reviews, surveys, in-app prompts, analytics, and insights from our product operations team and account executives.

One of the most valuable lessons I’ve learned is the importance of embracing all types of feedback—positive and negative. While you can’t design a product to satisfy every single use case, it’s essential to listen to a wide range of voices. This allows you to design solutions with enough flexibility to cater to different user types without over-complicating the experience.
